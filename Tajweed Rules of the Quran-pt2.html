2 Tafkheem & Tarqeeq (Velarization & Attenuation)

In Tajweed Part 2, letters are classified into 3 main divisions regarding heaviness (تَفْخِيم) and lightness (تَرْقِيق).

1

Always Mufakhkham

The 7 letters of Isti'laa: خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ. They are ALWAYS heavy under all circumstances!

2

Heavy / Light Conditions

3 Letters: Alif Madd (ا), Laam of Ism Allah (ل), and Ra' (ر).

3

Always Muraqqaq

All remaining letters of Istifaal (low tongue) are ALWAYS light!

3 Relationships Between Two Meeting Letters (علاقات الحروف)

When two letters meet in pronunciation, their relationship falls under one of 4 distinct categories based on whether they share the same Makhraj (articulation point) and Sifaat.

Select Two Consecutive Letters to Check Relationship:

Al-Mutamaathilaan (المتماثلان)

Two identical letters meeting with exact same Makhraj and Sifaat.

Sagheer Rule: Full Idgham (الإدغام) Example: ٱضْرِب بِّعَصَاكَ

1. Mutamaathilaan (المتماثلان)

Identical in Makhraj & Sifaat. (e.g. ب + ب)

2. Mutajaanisaan (المتجانسان)

Same Makhraj, different Sifaat. (e.g. ت + د)

3. Mutaqaaribaan (المتقاربان)

Close in Makhraj & Sifaat. (e.g. ل + ر)

4. Mutaba'idaan (المتباعدان)

Far apart in Makhraj & Sifaat. Rule is ALWAYS Izhar.

4 An-Nabr (النَّبْر - Phonetic Accent)

An-Nabr is a pressure or emphasis placed on a specific letter or syllable so that its sound is heard clearly without dropping letters or distorting meaning.

Case #1 of An-Nabr

Stopping on a Letter with Tashdeed (الوقف على المشدد)

When stopping on a letter with Shaddah (e.g. ٱلْحَيّ), you must place an accent on the stressed letter so the second hidden saakin letter isn't lost.
