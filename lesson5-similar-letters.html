Similar Letters (الحروف المتشابهة)
In Writing & In Pronunciation (كتابة ونطقًا)

💡
Two kinds of “similar”: some letters look alike on paper (only the dots differ), while others sound alike to a new learner even though Arabic treats them as completely different letters, each with its own articulation point (Makhraj) and qualities (Sifat). This lesson covers both.
Part 1 · Letters Similar in Writing (الحروف المتشابهة كتابة)
Same skeleton, different dots. Look closely at how many dots, and where.
بَ·نَ
Same curved base — ب has one dot BELOW, ن has one dot ABOVE.
تَ·يَ
ت has two dots ABOVE; ي has two dots BELOW (and a curved tail).
ثَ·شَ
ث = three dots above a ت-shaped base; ش = three dots above a wide س-shaped base.
جَ·خَ
Same hook shape — ج has one dot BELOW, خ has one dot ABOVE.
زَ·رَ
Same curved stroke — ز has one dot ABOVE, ر has no dot.
ذَ·دَ
Same shape — ذ has one dot ABOVE, د has no dot.
ضَ·صَ
Same loop shape — ض has one dot ABOVE, ص has no dot.
ظَ·طَ
Same loop shape — ظ has one dot ABOVE, ط has no dot.
غَ·عَ
Same hook shape — غ has one dot ABOVE, ع has no dot.
شَ·سَ
Three-tooth base — ش has three dots ABOVE, س has none.
فَ·قَ
Same round body — ف has one dot ABOVE, ق has two dots ABOVE.
لَ·كَ
Both tall vertical letters — look closely at the extra inner stroke in ك.
ةَ·هَ
ة (Ta Marbuta) is simply ه with two dots added above — only appears word-final.
أَ·لَ
Both are tall vertical strokes — أ carries the hamza mark on top.
عَ·غَ
Same hook shape — غ has one dot ABOVE, ع has none.
ةَ·هَ
ة (Ta Marbuta) is simply ه with two dots added above — only appears word-final.
نَ·ذَ
Different families — compare the dot: ن has it inside the curve, ذ sits above a د shape.
فَ·غَ
Compare carefully — ف has one dot above a round body, غ has one dot above a hooked body.
عَ·غَ
Same hook shape — غ has one dot ABOVE, ع has none.
ءَ·عَ
ء (Hamzah alone) is a small standalone mark; ع is a full hooked letter body.
Part 2 · Letters Similar in Pronunciation (الحروف المتشابهة نطقًا)
These sound alike to a new ear — but each has its own Makhraj (articulation point) and Sifat (characteristics).
The Three S-Sounds (السين والصاد والثاء)
سَ صَ ثَ
سَ
Seen
Makhraj (Articulation Point): Tip of the tongue, just behind the lower front teeth.
Sifat (Characteristics): Light (Isti’faal), whispered (Hams), continuous (Rakhawah).
Tongue & Mouth Tip: A thin, crisp “s” — like the English s in “see”. Keep the tongue low and relaxed.
سَمَك “fish”
صَ
Sad
Makhraj (Articulation Point): Same spot as Seen, but the BACK of the tongue rises toward the roof of the mouth.
Sifat (Characteristics): Heavy/emphatic (Isti’laa & Itbaaq), whispered (Hams), continuous (Rakhawah).
Tongue & Mouth Tip: A thick, rounded “S” — the mouth feels fuller. Raise the back of the tongue while the tip stays in the Seen position.
صَبْر “patience”
ثَ
Tha
Makhraj (Articulation Point): Tip of the tongue placed lightly BETWEEN the front teeth.
Sifat (Characteristics): Light (Isti’faal), whispered (Hams), continuous (Rakhawah).
Tongue & Mouth Tip: Like the English th in “think”. The tongue must be visible between the teeth.
ثَعْلَب “fox”
⚠ Common Mistake: Learners often flatten all three into one English “s” sound. Practice by exaggerating: keep ث between the teeth, keep س thin and flat, and puff the cheeks slightly for ص.
Light T vs. Heavy T (التاء والطاء)
تَ طَ
تَ
Ta
Makhraj (Articulation Point): Tip of the tongue against the ridge behind the upper front teeth.
Sifat (Characteristics): Light (Isti’faal), whispered (Hams), stopped (Shiddah).
Tongue & Mouth Tip: A crisp, light “t” like in English “top”.
تُفَّاح “apple”
طَ
Ta (heavy)
Makhraj (Articulation Point): Exact same tongue position as ت, but the BACK of the tongue rises and the mouth rounds.
Sifat (Characteristics): Heavy/emphatic (Isti’laa & Itbaaq), voiced (Jahr), stopped (Shiddah).
Tongue & Mouth Tip: A deep, full “T” that seems to come from further back. Round your lips slightly and press the tongue up.
طَبِيب “doctor”
⚠ Common Mistake: Saying ط exactly like an English “t” with no heaviness. Practice by rounding the lips and raising the back of the tongue while keeping the tip in place.
Light D vs. The Unique Dad (الدال والضاد)
دَ ضَ
دَ
Dal
Makhraj (Articulation Point): Tip of the tongue against the ridge behind the upper front teeth.
Sifat (Characteristics): Light (Isti’faal), voiced (Jahr), stopped (Shiddah).
Tongue & Mouth Tip: A simple, light “d” like in English “door”.
دَرْس “lesson”
ضَ
Dad
Makhraj (Articulation Point): The SIDE edge of the tongue (one or both sides) presses against the upper back molars — a makhraj unique to Arabic.
Sifat (Characteristics): Heaviest letter in the alphabet (Isti’laa & Itbaaq), voiced (Jahr), continuous (Rakhawah).
Tongue & Mouth Tip: Known as the letter that gives Arabic its nickname, “Lughat-ud-Daad”. Press the SIDE of your tongue, not the tip, against your upper molars.
ضَيْف “guest”
⚠ Common Mistake: Substituting ض with د or ظ. Remind students it is a side-of-tongue sound, not a tip-of-tongue sound — the hardest letter for most learners.
Light TH vs. Heavy TH (الذال والظاء)
ذَ ظَ
ذَ
Dhal
Makhraj (Articulation Point): Tip of the tongue between the front teeth (same spot as ث, but voiced).
Sifat (Characteristics): Light (Isti’faal), voiced (Jahr), continuous (Rakhawah).
Tongue & Mouth Tip: Like the English th in “this”.
ذَهَب “gold”
ظَ
Dha (heavy)
Makhraj (Articulation Point): Same interdental spot as ذ, but the back of the tongue rises — heavy version.
Sifat (Characteristics): Heavy/emphatic (Isti’laa & Itbaaq), voiced (Jahr), continuous (Rakhawah).
Tongue & Mouth Tip: Say ذ but fill the mouth and raise the back of the tongue at the same time.
ظُهْر “noon”
⚠ Common Mistake: Pronouncing ظ exactly like ذ with no extra heaviness. Practice the pair back-to-back to feel the difference.
Throaty H vs. Soft H (الحاء والهاء)
حَ هَ
حَ
Ha
Makhraj (Articulation Point): Middle of the throat, with a tight constriction.
Sifat (Characteristics): Whispered (Hams), continuous (Rakhawah), light (Isti’faal) but throat-tensed.
Tongue & Mouth Tip: Tighten your throat as if fogging up a mirror, but keep it voiceless. Much stronger than an English “h”.
حَليب “milk”
هَ
Ha (soft)
Makhraj (Articulation Point): The very bottom/outermost part of the throat — the most open, relaxed articulation point.
Sifat (Characteristics): Whispered (Hams), continuous (Rakhawah), very light.
Tongue & Mouth Tip: A soft, breathy “h”, exactly like the English “h” in “hello”.
هَدِيَّة “gift”
⚠ Common Mistake: Pronouncing ح like a plain English “h” with no throat tension. Practice tightening the throat muscles for ح while keeping ه relaxed.
Front K vs. Deep Q (الكاف والقاف)
كَ قَ
كَ
Kaf
Makhraj (Articulation Point): Back of the tongue against the soft palate — roughly where English “k” is made.
Sifat (Characteristics): Light (Isti’faal), whispered (Hams), stopped (Shiddah).
Tongue & Mouth Tip: A regular, light “k” like in English “kite”.
كِتَاب “book”
قَ
Qaf
Makhraj (Articulation Point): Further back — the base of the tongue against the uvula, deep in the throat.
Sifat (Characteristics): Heavy (Isti’laa), voiced (Jahr), stopped (Shiddah).
Tongue & Mouth Tip: A deep, hollow “k” that seems to come from the very back of the throat.
قَلَم “pen”
⚠ Common Mistake: Pronouncing ق like a regular English “k”. Push the sound further back, almost like a gentle throat-pop.
